Happy Aidiladha

Hari Raya Aidiladha, also known as Hari Raya Haji or Eid al-Adha, reminds us of the story of Prophet Ibrahim (A.S.) and his complete obedience to Allah’s command. It is a day to reflect on sacrifice, sincerity, and our dependence on Allah.

“Indeed, my prayer, my worship, my life and my death are all for Allah, Lord of all the worlds.” (Surah Al-An’am: 162)

Pilgrims at Masjid al-Haram remind us that Aidiladha is deeply connected to the Hajj and the spirit of sacrifice.

Meaning & Significance of Aidiladha

Aidiladha is known as the “Day of Sacrifice”. It commemorates the moment when Prophet Ibrahim (A.S.) dreamt that Allah commanded him to sacrifice his son, Prophet Ismail (A.S.). Both father and son submitted wholeheartedly, but Allah replaced Ismail with a ram, showing that the true test was obedience, not the blood or flesh of the sacrifice.

From this story, we learn:

Obedience to Allah

True faith is not just in words, but in the willingness to surrender what we love for the sake of Allah.

Sacrifice & Sincerity

The greatest sacrifice is letting go of ego, desires, and distractions that distance us from Allah.

Allah’s Love & Mercy

Allah does not need the meat or blood; what reaches Him is our piety and sincerity.

The Qurban & Sharing Blessings

One of the main practices of Aidiladha is the qurban, the sacrifice of animals such as goats, cows, or camels for those who are able. The meat is distributed to family, friends, and those in need, symbolising that sustenance is a trust from Allah that must be shared.

Through qurban, we:

  • Draw closer to Allah through a prescribed act of worship.
  • Strengthen bonds with family and neighbours.
  • Bring joy to the poor with meat they rarely get to enjoy.

Aidiladha Moments

Aidiladha is not only about the sacrifice, but also about prayer, remembrance of Allah, the takbir, and the atmosphere of gratitude shared with family.

Happy Hari Raya Aidiladha from Ghazali & Family. May our sacrifices be accepted and our hearts remain close to Allah.
